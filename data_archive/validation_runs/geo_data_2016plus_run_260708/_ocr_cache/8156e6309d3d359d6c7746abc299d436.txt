Executive summary Copper prices gathered pace in early December; fuelled, in part, by the encouraging rhetoric on economic policy for Chinafor 2025 and led to 3-month prices trading to a peak of USS9,314/t on 11 December their highest level since mid-November 2024. Prices have since come off to tradein alower range around the Uss9,ooolt level due to the lack of clarity around Chinese policy and its impact on copper demand in 2025. Dollar strength has also helped to temper prices over the final trading days of the month. Having cut interest rates for the third time this year, the US Fed has signalled a shallower rate of cuts in 2025 which could lead to volatility in the price. Global refined copper demand is estimated to increase by 3.296 in 2024, driven by the energy transition and the improving economic outlook. We expect further demand growth of 4.096 in 2025 and 2.996 in 2026. A broader economic recovery alongside the ramp up of semis capacity are expected to support our forecasts while demand will also be supported by the energy transition  However, the removal of supportive government policies in some countries may pose a downside risk while risks in theform of trade tariffs are a growing concern.
On 6 December, it was widely reported that Antofagasta had reached a settlement with Jiangxi Copper on the 2025 annual copper concentrate TCRCs at alevel of USS21.25/t and 2.125 cllb. Other members of the China Smelter Purchasing Team (CSPT) including Jinchuan; China Copper; Tongling and Daye are thought to have adopted these terms The figures are broadly in line with market expectations however they do represent a significant year-on-year decrease from the 2024 TCRC benchmark level of USs8o/t & 8.Oc/lb. The tightness in the spot concentrate marketis expected to continue in 2025, driven by ongoing expansions in primary smelter capacity both in China and elsewhere. The expected ramp up of new integrated smelters in Indonesia and DR Congo next year will also remove concentrate that was previously available to the custom market.
Since our last short-term outlook, we have lowered our mine supply and increased refined consumption forecasts, the combination of which has resultedin a smaller metal market surplus for 2024, and a projected ~270 kt metal market deficit for 2025, with refined stocks falling to 53 days of consumption As a result; copper prices are setto rise to an annual average of USs9,913/t (USS4.5O/Ib) in 2025.
