Prices Erratic copper Copper prices have remained erratic, trading in a USs5oO/t range over the past month. Since hitting a high of USS8,704.5o/t on 31 January, three-month copper prices come under pressure and by 9 February had slipped to USs8,127/t their lowest level since Noverber 2023. Data showing the biggest fall in Chinese consumer prices since the global financial crisis exacerbated near-term demand concerns and dragged prices lower, as did the modest 18 ktincreasein ShFE stocks. Twenty-one consecutive days of LME stock declines totalling 36.8 kt through to 22 February; helped copper recoup some of its losses over the period. Although three-month prices traded back above the USs8,6oo/t level by 22 February in anticipation of the return of Chinese buyers, the LME cash to 3-month spread remained ina wide contango of between USs78-114/t. This suggests ample supply in the nearby physical market. Some of the advance in base metal prices mid-month was also attributable to nervousness in the market ahead of an announcement of possible sanctions on Russian metals. Despite initial speculation both the US and the EU decided to hold-off on direct sanctions on Russian origin metal. However the dooris open to further EU sanctions laterin the year. Western buyers have largely shunned Russian metal over the past year with sales made to Asian countries and China in particular. If sanctions were to be imposed on Russian copper now, we suspect that the impact would not materially affect the physical market given European consumers have sourced material from elsewhere over the past year.
